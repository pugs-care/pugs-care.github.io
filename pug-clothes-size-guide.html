Pug Clothes That Actually Fit: How to Measure Your Pug and Choose the Right Size

Ask any group of pug owners about pug clothes and you will hear the same story: the sweater that fits the chest hangs past the tail, and the one with the right length will not close over the ribs. If that sounds like your house, you are in good company, and the fix is mostly a tape measure and a different way of reading size charts.

Quick answer: Measure three things while your pug stands: neck girth, chest girth just behind the front legs, and back length from the base of the neck to the base of the tail. Choose the size by chest first, check the neck second, and accept that the length will often run long. Owners in the r/pugs community report the same pattern: they size up and hem, or look for cuts labelled "wide".

Reference chest and back sizes come from one published retailer chart. Every brand cuts differently, so treat the result as a way to read your own numbers, then check them against the chart of the item you are buying.

Before you grab the tape, it helps to see the three measurements done on a real dog. In this vet-presented measuring guide from Pet Circle, you will see the length taken from the start of the neck to the start of the tail, the chest wrapped behind the front legs, and the neck measured with two fingers under the tape.

The video uses a longer-bodied dog, which is exactly why it is useful: watch where the tape starts and stops, then picture how much shorter that same line is on a pug with the same chest.

Why don't standard dog clothes fit a pug?

Most dog clothes are graded on a single body shape, so the chest and the back grow together from size to size. A pug breaks that assumption: a deep, wide ribcage sits on a short back with a thick, short neck, so one measurement lands in one size and the next measurement lands in another.

The chart was built for a different dog

Look at how a mainstream chart moves. The PetSmart dog clothes sizing chart lists Medium as an 8 inch neck, 13 inch back and 19 inch chest, and names the Pug as an example breed for that size, next to the Dalmatian. Small is a 6.5 inch neck, 9 inch back and 15 inch chest; Large is 10, 17 and 24 inches. Each step adds roughly four inches of back for every four to five inches of chest.

A pug that needs the Medium chest does not necessarily have the Medium back, and the neck is a separate question again. That mismatch is why so many of us end up with a garment that technically "fits" while bunching at the rump or riding up the throat.

Weight tells you almost nothing about fit

It is tempting to shop by pounds, but two pugs of the same weight can carry it very differently. Our look at adult pug weight against the AKC standard shows how widely owner-reported weights spread around the 14–18 lb breed ideal. A scale reading cannot give you a chest girth, so leave weight out of the size decision unless the brand's chart offers nothing else.

What this means when you shop

Expect compromise. The realistic goal is a garment that closes comfortably around the chest and neck, lets the front legs move freely, and is no longer than you can tolerate or hem. Getting all three numbers to match one size is the exception for this breed, not the rule.

Which measurements matter for pug clothes?

Three: back length, chest girth and neck girth. The American Kennel Club's guide to measuring dog gear says most dog clothing relies on these three, and that you should measure while your dog is standing so the spine is aligned and the weight is evenly spread.

Chest girth: the number that decides

Wrap the tape around the largest part of the rib cage, usually just behind the front legs. On a pug this is the measurement most likely to rule out a size, because a garment that will not close over the ribs cannot be fixed at home. Slip two fingers under the tape, as AKC advises for every circumference, so the number reflects a comfortable fit rather than a squeeze.

Neck girth: short but thick

Measure around the base of the neck, where a collar sits. Many pug owners joke that their dog's head and neck are almost the same width, and one owner in the community thread described their pug as having a "1:1 head to neck ratio". That matters for pull-over sweaters: the head has to pass through the opening, and then the opening has to sit comfortably at the base of the neck.

Back length: the one you can compromise on

Measure along the top of the spine from the base of the neck to the base of the tail. Keep the tape on the back rather than following it down over the hips. On most pugs this number will point to a smaller size than the chest does, and that is normal.

How to measure your pug step by step

Set aside five minutes, a soft sewing tape and a handful of treats. A second person helps, but it is not essential if your pug will stand for a lick of something tasty.

  1. Use a soft tape, not a metal one. If you only have string, mark it and lay it against a ruler afterwards.
  2. Get your pug standing squarely. A sitting or curled-up pug will give you a shorter back and a different chest number. Reward the stand, then measure.
  3. Measure the chest first. Go around the widest part of the ribs just behind the front legs, with two fingers under the tape. Write it down immediately.
  4. Measure the neck. Go around the base of the neck where a collar would sit, again with two fingers underneath.
  5. Measure the back. Start at the base of the neck and run the tape along the spine to where the tail begins.
  6. Repeat each measurement once. If the two readings differ, take the larger chest and neck figure and the shorter back figure, since those are the safer directions to err in.
  7. Note the unit and the date. Puppies change quickly, and a card that says "chest 19 in, March" is far more useful in autumn than a number you half remember.
  8. Run the numbers through the checker above, then compare them with the chart of the exact item you want to buy, not with a different brand's chart.

If your pug wears a harness under a coat, measure with the harness on as well and keep both numbers. Some coats have a harness opening; others need the extra room around the chest.

Which sizes do pug owners actually buy?

There is no single pug size. In a 2025 r/pugs thread asking which clothing brands actually fit pugs, owners reported everything from standard pet-store sizes to L/XL, and the common thread was measuring and adjusting rather than trusting the label.

The "Clothes for Pugs? Any brands that actually fit them correctly?" discussion, posted in November 2025, drew 43 comments. It is a small, self-selected group, but it is honest about what works in real homes.

Why the reports vary so much

Owners are describing different garments from different brands on pugs of different builds. A 14 lb pug and a broad 24 lb pug are both "pugs" in the thread, and a hoodie with stretch behaves nothing like a lined parka. That is exactly why your own three numbers beat any "pugs wear a medium" rule.

Sweater, coat or harness: which measurement leads?

Chest girth leads for almost everything, but the second measurement changes with the garment. The table below shows what to check first and what you can afford to compromise on.

Harnesses deserve extra care

For harnesses, AKC's guide names chest girth and neck girth as the most important measurements and recommends leaving enough room that the harness will not become too tight if your dog pulls. Flat-faced breeds are also why most pug people walk on a harness rather than a collar: in PetMD's article on harnesses for flat-faced breeds, veterinarian Jennifer Coates names the pug among them, notes that a collar only adds strain to their airways, and points out that head halters do not work well around prominent eyes and facial folds. The same article suggests mesh harnesses for breathability.

Coats: watch the front legs

The Pet Circle video sums up the length problem neatly: a coat that is too long rubs under the front legs, and one that is too short does little. On a pug, "too long" is the more common failure, so check the armpits after the first walk.

What should you do when your pug is between sizes?

Go up, then fix the length. AKC's advice is to opt for the larger size when in doubt, especially between sizes, and that is doubly true for a barrel-chested breed because a tight chest cannot be let out at home.

Ways owners make the larger size work

  • Hem it. Sleeves and body length are the easiest parts of a garment to shorten, as one owner in the thread does routinely.
  • Look for a wide or short cut. Some ranges offer broader or shorter variants inside the same letter size.
  • Choose stretch for pull-overs. Knits and fleece adapt to a deep chest better than a stiff woven shell.
  • Prefer adjustable closures for coats. A hook-and-loop chest strap lets one coat fit a pug who is between sizes.

When not to size up

If the chest already fits and only the neck is snug, sizing up the whole garment can leave the body swimming. Look instead for a wider or adjustable neckline, or a design that fastens rather than pulls over. The same flat-face design problem shows up at the food bowl, which is why we compared purpose-built options in our Fluff Trough review for pugs.

Common mistakes when buying pug clothes

Most returns come from a handful of avoidable errors. You will recognize some of these from your own closet; most of us have made them.

  • Buying by breed name. A chart that says "Pug = Medium" describes an average dog the brand had in mind, not yours.
  • Buying by weight. Weight cannot tell you chest girth or back length.
  • Measuring a sitting dog. Sitting shortens the back and shifts the chest. Measure standing, as AKC recommends.
  • Pulling the tape tight. Without two fingers of room, the garment will be snug before your pug even moves.
  • Using one brand's chart for another brand. Letter sizes are not standardized between brands.
  • Ignoring the neck on pull-over styles. A short, thick neck and a narrow opening make dressing a wrestling match.
  • Keeping a puppy's measurements. Re-measure growing pugs before every purchase.
  • Skipping the first-wear check. Watch your pug walk, sit and lie down in the new piece, and check under the front legs afterwards.

Pug clothes FAQ

What size clothes does a pug usually wear?

There is no universal pug size. One large retailer's chart lists the Pug under Medium (8 inch neck, 13 inch back, 19 inch chest), while owners report wearing anything from regular pet-store sizes to L/XL depending on the brand and the dog. Measure your pug and use the chart of the item you are buying.

Which measurement matters most for pug clothes?

Chest girth. AKC notes that most clothing relies on back length, chest girth and neck girth, and for a barrel-chested pug the chest is usually the measurement that decides whether a garment fits at all.

Should I size up if my pug is between sizes?

Yes, in most cases. AKC advises choosing the larger size when in doubt. Then shorten what is too long or choose a wide or adjustable cut.

How much room should I leave when measuring?

AKC suggests slipping two fingers between the tape and your dog when measuring circumferences such as the neck and chest.

Why are pug clothes always too long?

Most garments are graded so that chest and back grow together. Pugs combine a broad chest with a short back, so the size that fits the chest is often several inches longer than the pug's back.

Can my pug wear children's clothes?

Some owners do use toddler sizes, as one household in the community thread mentioned. Measure the chest, neck and back first and check that the leg openings and length suit a four-legged body.

The short version for pug people

Pug clothes that actually fit start with three honest numbers taken on a standing dog: chest, neck and back. Size by the chest, make sure the neck is comfortable, and treat extra length as something to hem rather than a reason to squeeze into a smaller size. Keep your measurement card handy, compare it with each brand's own chart, and you will waste far fewer returns than the rest of us did learning this the hard way.
