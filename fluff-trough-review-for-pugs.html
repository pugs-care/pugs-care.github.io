Fluff Trough Review for Pugs (2026): Elevated Flat-Face Feeder vs Leash Boss Flat Face Slow Feeder

If you own a pug, you have probably watched kibble skate to the far rim of a round bowl while your dog pushes the whole dish across the kitchen with its face. This Fluff Trough review for pugs compares the raised, trough-shaped Fluff Trough (in its standard and Binge Blocker versions) against the much cheaper Leash Boss Flat Face Feeder, a tilted plastic slow-feed dish. We look at specs, sizing for a typical pug, cleaning, cost over time and what owners actually report, so you can pick the one that matches your dog's eating style rather than the one with the loudest marketing.

Quick answer: Choose the Fluff Trough Silicone Insert Set ($55.50) if your pug chases food around the bowl or shoves the dish across the floor and you want a raised, wall-friendly trough. Choose the Fluff Trough Binge Blocker Set ($59.50) if you want that same raised base plus shallow slow-feed pockets. Choose the Leash Boss Flat Face Feeder ($19.99) if your main goal is slowing down a fast eater on a budget and you are happy with a flat, tilted plastic dish at floor level.

Not sure which camp your pug is in? Answer six quick questions below and the picker scores all three feeders against your dog's size, meal size, eating speed and your budget.

Both brands aim at the same problem: flat-faced dogs have short muzzles, so deep round bowls and tall slow-feeder mazes can be awkward to eat from. Where they differ is in how they solve it. The Fluff Trough lifts a long, open-fronted silicone tray off the floor; the Leash Boss keeps things low and flat, tilting a shallow maze plate toward the dog.

The one difference that matters most

The Fluff Trough is a feeding station: a plastic base that holds a removable insert above the floor. The Leash Boss is a single dish: one piece of molded plastic, half an inch tall, with a tilt built into it. That changes everything downstream, from how much floor space it takes to how much you pay to replace a worn part.

Fluff Trough Silicone Insert Set review

The standard set is the product most people mean when they say "Fluff Trough." For $55.50 you get the base, one silicone insert and four non-slip feet, in black, gray, purple or teal. The insert lifts out for washing, so you rinse a floppy silicone tray rather than scrubbing the whole stand. Fluff Trough says it is top-rack dishwasher safe and microwave safe and works with dry, wet or raw food. A spare insert costs $19.99 if you want one in the dishwasher while the other is in use.

The shape is the selling point for pugs. Instead of a round bowl with a deep center, you get a wide, open-fronted trough with a back wall, and the company suggests pushing it against a wall for extra stability if your dog tends to shove its dishes. On the brand's own store, this set shows a 4.96 out of 5 average from 405 reviews. Keep in mind those are reviews collected by the seller, not an independent panel.

Real-world feedback from pug owners on a popular r/pugs thread about a new Fluff Trough is mostly enthusiastic, with a few useful details mixed in. One owner of an 11-month-old, 23 lb pug standing about 12 inches at the shoulder went with the regular size over the XL, partly because the XL did not offer the stainless steel insert at the time. Another joked that their pug tries to eat from the back of the trough, which is a fair reminder that no shape fully overrides a pug's own ideas.

Pros

  • Raised, open-fronted trough that keeps food in one long area instead of a deep round well.
  • Removable silicone insert is easy to wash; spare inserts are sold separately.
  • Non-slip feet, plus the option to wedge it against a wall for dogs that push dishes.
  • Optional leg extensions if you want it higher.
  • Holds 2 cups, far more than a pug's typical meal.

Cons

  • Almost three times the price of the Leash Boss.
  • Not a slow feeder on its own; a fast eater will still finish quickly.
  • Bulkier and heavier than a simple dish (the company notes higher international shipping for that reason).
  • Returns are accepted within 30 days, but return shipping is at your own cost.

The stainless steel option

Fluff Trough also sells a Stainless Steel Set at $77.50, using the same base with a metal insert instead of silicone. It costs $22 more than the silicone set. Some owners prefer steel for water or simply for the feel, and the r/pugs thread shows the steel inserts can be hard to get when stock runs low.

Fluff Trough Binge Blocker Set review

The Binge Blocker is the same raised base and feet with a different silicone insert: tiered rows of shallow food pockets instead of a smooth tray. It costs $59.50, only $4 more than the standard set. Fluff Trough says the pockets hold about 1 cup of food, with space for roughly another cup, and that they are intentionally shallower than traditional slow feeders built for longer-snouted dogs. The insert is made in the USA from silicone and, like the standard one, goes on the top rack of the dishwasher.

On the brand store it averages 4.92 out of 5 from 151 reviews. The most useful reviews are the mixed ones. One owner who loves the height and shape wrote that their pug still "wipes out his food in record timing" and wished the bowl had more difficult "moguls." Another, a Frenchie owner, said over-eager dogs still try to eat too fast with or without the dish. In other words: the Binge Blocker slows things down, but it is designed to be gentle on a short face, so it is not the most challenging puzzle feeder you can buy.

Pros

  • Combines the raised trough with a slow-feed pattern in one set.
  • Shallow pockets designed for short faces, so food is less likely to get stuck.
  • Same base as the other Fluff Trough sets, so you can swap inserts (sold separately) within the same size.
  • Silicone is soft and dishwasher friendly.

Cons

  • Very determined gulpers may still finish fast, according to owner reviews.
  • Pockets mean more nooks to clean than the smooth insert.
  • Costs about three times as much as the Leash Boss dish.

Leash Boss Flat Face Feeder review

The Leash Boss Flat Face Feeder (listed on the brand site as the Flat Slow Feed Bowl) takes the opposite approach. It is a single plastic plate, 8 x 8 inches and half an inch deep, with a snout-friendly maze and a built-in tilt toward the dog. It holds up to 1.5 cups, works with wet or dry food, has non-slip feet and is dishwasher safe. The list price on the Leash Boss site is $19.99; when we checked, the blue version there showed as sold out, but it is also stocked by large pet retailers.

The product page carries 167 owner reviews, and the detailed ones are realistic. One owner of French bulldogs and English bulldogs said other slow feeders left food stuck in the crevices, while this one works for "squish faces," but it "does slow them down just a bit." Another said their French bulldog's meals went from 30 seconds to a minute to about 2.5 minutes. One reviewer simply wished it came in more colors. Those reviews also come from the seller's own site, so treat them as owner anecdotes, not measurements.

Pros

  • $19.99, the cheapest way to try a short-face slow feeder.
  • Flat, tilted maze is shallow enough for short muzzles.
  • One piece: nothing to assemble, easy to toss in the dishwasher.
  • Small footprint and light enough to pack for a weekend away.

Cons

  • Floor level only; no raised option.
  • Plastic rather than silicone or steel.
  • Slows eating "just a bit" for some dogs, according to one owner.
  • Limited color choice, and stock on the brand site can run out.

Binge Blocker vs Leash Boss: which slows a pug down more?

This is the real head-to-head, because both are marketed as slow feeders for flat faces. Nobody has published a timed test of the two side by side, and we won't invent one. What the specs and owner reports suggest is this:

  • Pattern: The Binge Blocker uses rows of separate pockets; the Leash Boss uses a continuous shallow maze on a slope. Both are deliberately shallow.
  • Owner results: Both have owners saying meals got longer, and both have owners saying a very determined dog still eats quickly. One Leash Boss reviewer reported meals stretching to about 2.5 minutes; one Binge Blocker reviewer wanted harder "moguls."
  • Height: Only the Binge Blocker is raised. If you specifically want a raised slow feeder, the Leash Boss is not a substitute.
  • Cost to experiment: At $19.99, the Leash Boss is a cheap first test. If it does not suit your dog, you have lost far less than with a $59.50 set.

A practical way to decide: time your pug's current meal with a phone stopwatch for three days. If meals are already over three minutes, you probably need a better shape (the standard Fluff Trough) more than a slow feeder. If your pug finishes in under a minute, a slow-feed surface of either kind is the priority, and the Leash Boss is the cheaper way to see whether a shallow pattern is enough.

Sizing: which Fluff Trough fits a pug?

Fluff Trough makes two base sizes, standard and XL, and describes its standard sets as intended for small to medium dogs and cats. The American Kennel Club's pug breed profile lists pugs at 10 to 13 inches tall and 14 to 18 pounds, which sits comfortably in that small-dog range. Most pugs will use the standard size; the owner on Reddit with a 23 lb pug also stayed with the regular trough.

Capacity is not a concern for pugs either. A pug's meal is usually well under the 2-cup capacity of the standard insert and the 1.5-cup capacity of the Leash Boss. Where capacity does matter is the Binge Blocker: its pockets hold about 1 cup, so if you feed a larger single meal, some food will sit on top of the pockets rather than in them, which reduces the slow-feed effect.

If you are unsure, Fluff Trough publishes a size guide on its site and gives an email address for sizing questions. Before ordering spare inserts, check that they are listed for your base size (standard or XL).

Cleaning and everyday use

Fluff Trough

Daily cleaning is the insert only. Lift it out, rinse or put it on the top rack, and wipe the base occasionally. Because the base stays on the floor and the insert is soft silicone, owners can keep a second insert in rotation for $19.99. The open front makes it easy to see when food is left in a corner, which also helps if you track how much your pug actually eats in a day.

Leash Boss

One piece, dishwasher safe, done. A maze dish does trap a bit more wet food in its channels than a smooth tray, so a quick pre-rinse helps before it goes in the machine. One owner noted they simply put it on the top rack.

Floor space

The Leash Boss is an 8-inch square plate. The Fluff Trough is a raised station with listed dimensions of 7.25 x 7.5 x 10 inches, so it needs a dedicated spot, ideally against a wall. If your pug eats in a crate or a small apartment corner, measure first. Our pug care guides cover more of the everyday setup around feeding.

Which one should you buy?

  • Your pug pushes the bowl across the room or chases kibble to the rim: Fluff Trough Silicone Insert Set. The shape and the option to set it against a wall are the reasons to pay more.
  • You want raised and slow in one product: Fluff Trough Binge Blocker Set. It is only $4 more than the standard set.
  • Your pug inhales food and you want the cheapest sensible test: Leash Boss Flat Face Feeder. Try it for two weeks and time meals before and after.
  • You travel with your pug: Leash Boss. A flat plate packs far more easily than a feeding station.
  • Your pug is larger than the breed standard: check the Fluff Trough size guide before ordering; many owners of bigger pugs still use the standard size.

Whatever you choose, give your pug a few days to adjust. New shapes and surfaces can confuse a dog at first, and a feeder that seems ignored on day one is often the favorite by day five.

Common mistakes when buying a feeder for a pug

  • Buying a deep standard slow feeder. Tall maze walls designed for longer snouts are exactly what flat-face feeders were made to avoid.
  • Overfilling the Binge Blocker. Food piled above the pockets is not slowed down. Split a large meal in two if needed.
  • Ignoring floor space. A raised trough needs a stable, ideally wall-backed spot.
  • Judging by brand-site star ratings alone. Both brands show reviews on their own stores. Read the three- and four-star comments for the real trade-offs.
  • Mixing sizes. Fluff Trough sells standard and XL bases. Check that any spare insert is listed for the size you own.

Fluff Trough and Leash Boss FAQ

Is the Fluff Trough worth it for a pug?

It is worth it if your pug's problem is the shape of the bowl: food sliding away, the dish moving, or awkward reach into a deep round bowl. At $55.50 it costs far more than a basic dish, so if your only goal is slowing a fast eater, try a cheaper flat slow feeder first.

What size Fluff Trough does a pug need?

Most pugs use the standard size. The AKC lists pugs at 14 to 18 pounds and 10 to 13 inches tall, and Fluff Trough says its standard sets are intended for small to medium dogs. One Reddit owner with a 23 lb pug also chose the regular size.

Is the Leash Boss Flat Face Feeder raised?

No. It is a flat plate about half an inch tall with a built-in tilt toward the dog. It sits on the floor and has non-slip feet.

Which slows a pug down more, the Binge Blocker or the Leash Boss?

No published side-by-side test exists. Owners of both report longer meals, and owners of both say very determined dogs still eat quickly. The Leash Boss is the cheaper experiment; the Binge Blocker adds the raised base.

Are both feeders dishwasher safe?

Yes. Fluff Trough says its silicone inserts are top-rack dishwasher safe and microwave safe. Leash Boss lists its Flat Face Feeder as dishwasher safe, and an owner review mentions using the top rack.

How much food fits in each feeder?

The Fluff Trough silicone insert holds 2 cups. The Binge Blocker pockets hold about 1 cup, with room for about another cup. The Leash Boss holds up to 1.5 cups. All of these are more than a typical pug meal.

Can I use the Fluff Trough for wet or raw food?

Yes. Fluff Trough says its silicone inserts work with dry, wet and raw food. The Leash Boss is listed for wet or dry food.

The bottom line on the Fluff Trough for pugs

The Fluff Trough is a well-built, raised feeding station whose real advantage for a pug is its shape: a long, open trough that keeps food reachable and stays put. The Binge Blocker version adds gentle slow-feed pockets for only $4 more. The Leash Boss Flat Face Feeder does one job, a shallow tilted slow-feed surface, at about a third of the price. Pick the Fluff Trough when the bowl itself is the problem, pick the Leash Boss when speed is the problem and budget matters, and time a few meals before and after so you know whether the switch actually worked for your dog.
